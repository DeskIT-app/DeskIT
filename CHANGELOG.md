## 1.0.5

My learned words can no longer be wiped out by a crash. If the computer lost power or ran out of disk while DeskIT was saving the words it had learned from my corrections, the list could come back empty the next time — and the account sync then deleted every one of those words from my other PCs as well. Now the list is saved whole or not at all, the last good copy is kept beside it, a damaged list is put aside and the good copy read in its place, and the sync deletes a word from my account only when I removed it on this PC; anything else that is missing is brought back down from the account instead. Two promises that were not being kept are kept now too: "keep what I dictated for 30 days" really trims the older lines on an installed copy (it failed in silence, because DeskIT was holding the file open while it tried, and still told its log the lines were gone), and unticking "Check for updates weekly" during setup really turns the weekly check off — before, the switch was ignored and DeskIT went on asking GitHub every week.

## 1.0.4

Settings is rearranged, and every tab is named for what you came looking for. I went looking for where my recordings and screenshots are saved and could not find them — they were under Screen, and they belong in General. There are eight tabs now: General (the dot and its corner, what is drawn on the screen, the microphone, Start with Windows, where files are saved, updates), Dictation (the speech model, what it learns, the translate key), Screen (screenshots, recordings, the camera), Messages & sounds, Phone, Account, Privacy and About. The words changed too: instead of library and code names it says what a thing does and how much room it takes — "Screen recording and the webcam need one extra download (28 MB)" instead of a library's name — all twenty sounds have human names ("Nothing to do", not "noop"), and facts about this computer and the model moved to About, where a fact belongs.

And Connect Claude Code now says which copy of DeskIT holds it, and asks before taking it. One PC has one Claude settings file, and the switch only asked whether anyone was named in it: on the installed copy it stood on while the lines pointed at another copy, and the cards went there. The switch is drawn from the file itself now — on when this copy holds the door, off with the other copy's folder in amber when it does not — and turning it on asks inside the card, "Disconnect the other DeskIT copy and connect this one?", before it writes. The wizard's extras row asks the same question.

## 1.0.3

After an update the app opens again, and the desktop icon stays where it was. On my first update that went through (2026-09-21) the app closed and did not reopen, and the icon jumped to another place on my desktop. The reason: the app leaves by itself before the installer runs, so Windows' way of bringing back what it closed had nothing to bring back — the installer now opens the app itself once the files are in place, and the first start says "Updated to DeskIT x". And the icon: the installer deleted and recreated it on every upgrade, and Windows put the new one in its next free cell — from now on an icon that is there stays put, one you deleted does not come back, and the uninstaller takes it either way.

An update is one button (2026-09-21, after my first update: only Download — I want everyone on the same version; we are not at the stage of letting people choose). ## 1.0.1

The account lock (2026-09-21 — I asked for it: encrypted, so the same account opens it on every PC and the server cannot read it). One key per account, made on the first PC and held only by that account's own PCs — the server keeps its fingerprint, never the key. What you said and the cloud keys (Groq, Gemini) are locked before they leave the PC and open only on a PC that holds the key; each row's time and kind stay readable so the syncs can order them. A new PC signing in shows an eight-character code and waits; on a PC already in, a notification arrives like any other (the corner card, the cue) whose click opens the desk on Home, where the request sits with the same code — compare, press Approve, and the key crosses to it wrapped (what Apple, Bitwarden and WhatsApp do); every other screen carries a banner while someone waits. No card asks by itself beside the dot. No other PC at hand? A recovery key the app generates (24 characters, shown once — Home offers it, and so does Settings > Privacy > The lock), and a new PC that types it is in. Until the approval the new PC works with the words and settings; what you said and the keys follow. The plaintext history that sat on the server was deleted and sent again locked; the history table lost its plaintext columns. The lock is Windows' own (CNG, AES-256-GCM), no new package. The two sync consent cards say so now; a consent given under the earlier words is carried forward without asking again — the new words promise less, not more.

The Corrections place is the whole word list now (2026-09-21, after I saw another app's dictionary page: a full list anyone can see; a way to add by hand, because the transcriber writes the same wrong thing every time and the app never proposes it; a search; and the switch). The Words tab lists every learned pair, newest first, with one plain line each (fixed on every dictation · corrected once · in the decoder's prompt), a search over both sides of a pair, a Teach a word button that opens a row — what it hears ⟶ what you mean — and a hand-typed pair is fixed on every dictation at once; the pencil on a row changes a pair in place, the cross takes it out, and out of the account's other PCs too; the Use them switch is vocab.enabled — off, the list stays but neither repairs nor reaches the prompt, and that holds from the next dictation rather than the next start. The second reading's proposals sit above the list ("Waiting on you") and push it down; with none waiting there is no section. The tab's Lately panel is gone (the Said page keeps its own).

The proposal itself is said the way Track Changes says it: the sentence cut around the change, the word as heard on a red-edged pill in its place in the sentence, an arrow, and the proposed word on a gold pill with a tick in it — on the desk, on the home and on the card beside the dot (the card grew to 460). On the old row (a pair beside the sentence) I said it is really hard to understand where the mistake was — maybe I meant that; and on a strike-through: hard to read — posh, I could also have said it is a U. The arrow points the way the sentence reads — right to left in Hebrew, the other way in English — and the tick is always next to the arrow; a Latin term inside a Hebrew sentence sits where the Hebrew reader meets it. A proposal with several changes draws them all in the sentence, and what does not fit the row is counted in the note.

Sync is live (2026-09-20 — I asked for it: I sync, and not two seconds pass and it is on the other copy). Every push ends with one signal on the account's private channel — the names of the stores that changed and the PC's id, not a word of what was said — and every signed-in copy holds one open connection on that channel (its own row on the Network screen, closed in offline mode) and pulls the named store the moment a signal from another PC lands. A dictation, a learned word, a saved setting: the wait after a change went from five seconds to half a second, and the 15-minute pass stays as the net underneath. The Account row in Settings says "live" while the channel is open, and its Sync now button is gone ("if it syncs all the time there is no need for a button"); a sync that was withdrawn comes back with Turn on on its own row under Settings > Privacy.

Two copies of one account showed two Said pages, two Lately lists and two Home screens (2026-09-20 afternoon). Three causes, three fixes: the history push had failed on every pass for two days — two "learned" events in the same second, and the server refused the whole batch (HTTP 500) — every row is now keyed to the millisecond, and two born in the same millisecond are a millisecond apart; the "what you said" sync was a separate switch nobody found — the sign-in and the wizard's one sync switch on the last page now cover it too ("Keep my words, settings and what I said in my account", on, with the line saying what leaves and how to turn it off), a copy already signed in gets it at its next start, and Settings > Privacy > Withdraw turns either of the two off on its own; and the desk did not see a row that arrived from the other PC until the next dictation here touched the file — it now watches the pulled file too. A word corrected on the other PC shows in Lately here as well.

The "Claude finished" card says the message in one sentence (2026-09-21 — I asked for it: sometimes the messages are long... let it understand the whole message and write one sentence there, it's much clearer). The hook now sends the whole message Claude finished with (up to 5,000 characters), Groq writes one Hebrew sentence about it, and the card waits for that sentence — at most a second ([notify] summary_wait_s; measured 0.2–0.4 s on messages of 136–3,700 characters) — and comes up with it in place of the first four lines. Claude's own turn is not slowed by a millisecond: the hook runs after it has finished. No Groq key, the cloud-text switch off, a late answer or an empty one: the old card, and nothing leaves. A model of its own (qwen3.8-27b), so a busy minute of finishes never costs the repair pass its quota; what is sent is the head and the tail of a long message, and the message itself is never stored. The cloud-text card now says this too — which is why it asks once more — not on a floating card but as a row on the desk's Home, in the "want an answer" pile: what changed in one line, where it goes and on whose key, and Turn on / Not now (until now a card whose words changed switched its feature off in silence, and the dictation fell to Ollama with no word why — my Dev, 2026-09-21 afternoon; the floating card that came up at start I refused the same day: every message inside the app). Settings > Privacy offers Turn on on such a row too. Settings > Messages from other programs > "Say a finished message in one line" turns it off. And a question with choices now lands as a "Claude asks you a question" card carrying the question itself — not "Claude needs your permission to use AskUserQuestion" (I said: it's reading the log, and the log says AskUserQuestion — fix it).

The dot moves from the panel, with a light round the screens (2026-09-21 — I asked for it: a button, and the moment you press it the tab closes, around every connected screen there is a halo in the app's colour, and I press and hold the dot and move it wherever I want; a Done button somewhere strategic, Enter finishes, Escape cancels). A small card at the top of the screen the dot is on: Done (or Enter) keeps it there, Esc puts it back where it was; left alone, the light goes out after three minutes and the dot stays. "Move the dot" in Settings now does the same. The light takes no clicks: everything under it keeps working.

The panel is a speech bubble anchored to the dot, and the Done card has a map (2026-09-21 evening, after my first try: "the tab opens very far from the dot", "the tab should move with it", "like a speech bubble — the corner nearest the dot sharpens into it", and "Options — hover it and a little screen opens with my screens and four dots, each corner; click one and the dot goes there"). On the Done card, Options opens on hover a map of the screens with a dot in every corner; a click puts the dot in that corner of that screen and ends the move — a corner is an answer, nothing to confirm.

Ask about the screen through Groq works again (2026-09-21). The Download and install button installs (2026-09-21, my first press, from 1.0.0: "Error creating log file" and the app closed without installing). ## 1.0.0

The first DeskIT — Hebrew dictation that stays on your PC. Two clicks to install, no administrator password; the installer downloads the Hebrew speech model itself (and, with an NVIDIA card, what makes transcription fast), so the first start is a short wizard for the microphone and the key. Hold a key, speak, the text lands where your cursor is. Signed in before on another PC? The first start says welcome back, brings your learned words and settings, and opens DeskIT — no wizard. Your voice never leaves the computer; what you said is kept in one folder you can delete; nothing is sent anywhere until you turn it on yourself, and every outgoing connection is listed on one screen. A drawn keys screen, learning from the words you corrected, screen recording and a photo key, translate, look up a word, and a phone that dictates through your own PC.

Your account: the first start asks — "Create an account" (a name, Google, and the rest of the setup) or "I have an account" (Google, and that is all: your learned words and settings come down and DeskIT opens by itself; only the downloads page if this PC still lacks something). Your learned words follow you by default — the wizard's last page says what leaves (the words and the settings; never your voice, your keys or what you said) and how to turn it off (Settings > Privacy > Withdraw). One double-click opens the desk with the speech model loading behind it; the Windows-logon start opens no window. Back in the wizard goes one step back; "Not you? Sign out" signs out this PC only.
